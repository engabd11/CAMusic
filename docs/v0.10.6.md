# v0.10.6 — the analyser learns to hear, and the layers stop littering

Two pieces of work. The first makes the offline track analysis substantially more
accurate, which every scan-driven show reads from. The second takes the
allocation and the recomputation out of the four creative light-show layers, and
fixes two places where they did not do what their own documentation said.

---

## The analyser: version 3

### The key detector was reading almost nothing

`TrackScan.key` is what `MusicDnaLayer` derives its entire anchor hue from, so if
the key is wrong the fingerprint is wrong. It was wrong.

There was no way to know that from the code or from the tests. The unit tests
check that the correlation recovers a synthetic key profile — which it does,
perfectly — and that is a test of the last step of a five-step process. So this
release also adds `tools/analysis-harness/`, which runs the real scan path over
real music and measures it on something that needs no ground truth: **transpose a
track up by three semitones and its detected key must move up by exactly three.**

The old detector scored **12.4 %** on that, against a 1-in-12 chance floor of
8.3 %. Three separate faults, each found by measuring rather than by reading:

1. **The chroma could not resolve a semitone across most of its range.** It
   assigned each FFT bin to its nearest semitone from 80 Hz up, but the analysis
   window is 46 ms, so a partial's main lobe is about 43 Hz wide — three and a
   half semitones at 200 Hz. No amount of careful weighting fixes that, because
   the energy genuinely is spread over three semitones. It now reads
   **interpolated spectral peaks**, which locate a partial to a fraction of a bin
   however wide its lobe is.

2. **It assumed A440.** Real records are not: this album sits 4–7 cents sharp and
   one track on it is 24 cents flat. The tuning offset is now measured — off the
   same peaks, as an amplitude-weighted circular mean of their deviation from the
   semitone grid — and divided out before the fold to twelve. (The first attempt
   measured it off bin *assignments* and reported the shape of the FFT's bin grid
   instead of the music, with a bias of over ten cents. The harness caught that
   too.)

3. **It was hearing the third harmonic and calling it the tonic.** With the first
   two fixed, the remaining failures were not random — they were *exactly +5
   semitones*, on eight separate tracks. Every note's third harmonic lands a fifth
   above it, so crediting each peak to its own pitch class manufactures a dominant
   nobody played. Each peak is now credited to every fundamental it could be a
   harmonic of, the standard HPCP treatment.

Also switched from Krumhansl-Kessler's probe-tone profiles to Temperley's, which
do not confuse a minor key with its relative major nearly as readily.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| shift consistency | 12.4 % | **71.1 %** |
| detune stability (±30 cents) | 72.7 % | **90.9 %** |
| median key confidence | 0.31 | 0.47 |
| tonics agreeing with librosa | 0 / 11 | **7 / 11** |

Two tracks still report a confidence near zero, which is the detector saying it
cannot tell. That is the honest answer for them, and Music DNA already weights how
hard the key anchors the room by exactly that number — so a coin-flip key barely
moves the lights.

### Sections now say which are the same

Segmentation found the boundaries but nothing said what was on either side of
them, so `MusicDnaLayer` stepped its hue by section *index*: every section a new
colour, the second chorus different from the first, and on a long track the
anchor walked right round the wheel and wrapped onto its own intro.

Sections now carry a `label`, from clustering the block band profiles the
segmenter already computed and used to throw away. A returning chorus returns to
its own colour.

### The metre is measured rather than assumed

`beatsPerBar` was hardcoded to 4 in two places, so on anything in 3 the
"downbeat" landed on a different beat of each bar in turn and every bar-synced
effect in the engine walked round the bar instead of sitting on its one. It is now
detected from three signals — bass onset, harmonic change between beats, and
section-boundary alignment — with an asymmetric margin, because four is
overwhelmingly the prior and calling a 4/4 track a waltz is the worse error.
`SyncoEngine` gains a 3/4 bar-weight table, since a waltz is not a 4/4 bar with a
beat lopped off.

### Beat times are no longer quantised to the hop

The DP can only place a beat on a frame, so every beat time was rounded to 20 ms —
visible in a scan report as every BPM being an exact multiple of `60/(k·0.02)`. A
parabola through the onset envelope recovers the real position, clamped to ±½ a
frame so a beat can never cross its neighbour.

### Storage

Scan file format 4, appending metre, tuning and section labels after the format-3
key — append-only, as every bump has been, so existing scans keep loading.
`ANALYSER_VERSION` goes to 3, which flags stored scans for the re-read that gives
them a key worth having. Nothing is deleted; the analysis card offers the refresh.

## The light-show layers

### They were allocating hard on the 60 Hz render loop

Every layer ended in `base.mapValues { … }` — a fresh `LinkedHashMap` per layer
per frame — and inside it `rgbToHsv` allocated a `FloatArray(3)` per lamp. With
four layers on and ten channels that is roughly 240 maps and 2,400 short-lived
arrays a second, on the one thread in this app with a hard deadline.

`LayerChain` now owns two output maps and alternates between them, and the HSV
round trip writes into a buffer each layer keeps for its own lifetime. Two more
things were being recomputed for answers that had not changed: Phantom Stage
re-summed the same melbank slice (and allocated a `Pair`) once per lamp for a
value identical across the room, and the Conductor recomputed each lamp's
colour-axis projection and ring azimuth every frame although both are pure
functions of a position that changes once per stream.

### Music DNA was not actually deterministic

Its drift phase was integrated from `dt`, so the colour at a given second
depended on how the playhead got there — a seek, a pause, or simply starting from
a different point gave a different room. That contradicts the one thing the
feature claims. The phase now comes from the track position, which deletes the
state rather than adding any.

### The Conductor's spin gate chattered

`rotationRateZ` is the single most recent gyroscope sample, and it was tested
against one threshold — so a wrist resting near that threshold flipped `spinning`
every frame, and the spin envelope, whose whole purpose is a 0.3 s ramp, spent its
life reversing instead of reaching either end. There is now a short EMA on the
rate and a lower rate to stop at than to start at.

### Room gestures: less work on the audio thread

`GestureTracker`'s did-it-come-back test walks the entire stored history, and was
being run for every (bin, window) pair — sixteen bins by four windows, ten times a
second — when the great majority of those candidates had already lost to a better
window of the same bin. It now runs only for a candidate that would otherwise win,
which cannot change the outcome.

## TTS: the tail fix from 0.10.5, actually working this time

0.10.5 set out to stop announcements losing their last words and made them worse:
the clip now stops right after the pre-announcement tone, before the sentence has
begun. Four separate faults, and they compound.

### The discriminator never ran

Everything 0.10.5 added hangs off one collector, which reads the player state out
of Music Assistant's `player_updated` event. It read `state`; a serialised MA
player carries **`playback_state`** — the key `MaParse.players` in this same app
has always read. Every event fell out at the elvis, so `lastPlayerState` and
`remotePausedAtMs` were never written at all.

With no readings, `StreamClassifier` answered `UNKNOWN` for every stream, which is
treated as music. No announcement was ever recognised as one — so it got the 400 ms
music tail cap instead of 5 s, and never got the speech `AudioAttributes` the
ducking fix was written to apply. `StreamEndPolicy` never saw a pause from another
controller either, so *every* `stream/end` took the "not a pause" branch.

### A drain outlives its own stream

Which is what turned a dead fix into a regression. A `stream/end` now starts a
drain coroutine that cuts the tail when its cap expires — and MA plays an
announcement by ending the music stream and starting the announcement a few hundred
milliseconds later, squarely inside the music tail's 400 ms cap. The drain then
called `cutTail()`, which acts on *whatever the engine is playing now*: it
discarded the announcement's queue and muted the player, a moment after its
pre-announcement tone.

Cancelling the job on `stream/start` does not fix this — `cancel()` is only
observed at a suspension point and there is none between the last check and the
cut. Every tail operation now names the stream it belongs to, and the engine drops
one addressed to a stream that has been superseded.

### The drain was watching the wrong thing

It exited as soon as the frame queue ran dry, which means the bytes reached
ExoPlayer — not that they were heard. Up to a second of audio is in the decoder and
the audio track at that moment, and the mute takes all of it. On a stream the server
paces, the queue is *already* empty when `stream/end` lands, so the drain broke on
its first poll and the behaviour was bit-for-bit what it replaced.

It now waits for the player's own end of playback (`STATE_ENDED`, which media3
reaches when the sink reports every frame written has been played). The caps go back
to being what they were documented as — a safety net, not the mechanism.

### `signalEndOfStream` was destroying the tail it was meant to save

On a grouped stream, `SendspinSyncDataSource.waitUntilDue` aborted a frame's
schedule the moment end-of-stream was signalled, and the caller's `continue` then
walked the rest of the queue dropping it a frame at a time. Since signalling the end
is *all* the drain path does, the drain could never drain anything there.

`stream/end` ("no more frames are coming") and `discardQueued` ("the tail is
abandoned") are now separate flags. Only the second aborts a schedule — which is
what a pause and a seek both go through, so pause latency is unchanged, and
`flush()` and `release()` now discard explicitly rather than relying on the
conflation.

### One event should not both identify an announcement and condemn it

MA pauses the queue to make room for a clip. That pause is what the classifier reads
to *call* the stream an announcement — and the same event stamped `remotePausedAtMs`,
which `StreamEndPolicy` then read as "the listener paused" and discarded the tail
for. It is ignored on both sides now: the collector does not stamp it while
`announcement_in_progress` is set, and the policy does not honour a remote pause
against an announcement. A *local* pause still stops one, because that is the
listener rather than the server.

### Classification

MA's own `announcement_in_progress` is read from `player_updated` and beats every
inference. Only `true` is acted on — MA sets it before it plays and clears it after,
so a fresh `true` is definitive while a `false` may not have caught up.

The inference stays for builds that do not send it, with its track-boundary rule
narrowed: "a stream that starts moments after one ended is the next track" rested on
MA not having republished the state yet, and that stops being true the moment a
reading exists which is *fresher* than the boundary. Applied unconditionally it
called every announcement the next track, since MA always starts one inside the
2 s linger.

## Verification

823 unit tests, green. New coverage for the peak-based chroma (a harmonic tone
lands on its own pitch class; the fifth its third harmonic manufactures does not
outvote it; a 30-cent detune is measured to within a few cents; a 40-cent-sharp
tone is still the note it is), the metre detector (harmony decides it when the
bass is flat; ties go to 4), beat refinement (ordered, in range, flat envelopes
left alone), section labelling, the chain's buffer reuse (including a
pass-through layer between two working ones), Music DNA's determinism across a
seek, the spin hysteresis, and a format-3 file still loading.

Measured against real music with `tools/analysis-harness` — not in CI, since the
corpus is somebody's music library. See its README for how to run it and for the
full record of what it found.

**Still needs a device:** the room itself. Nothing here changes what the base show
does, but Music DNA's returning-chorus colour and the Conductor's spin want eyes
on real lamps.

The TTS work needs one too, and more so — the whole of it is about what Music
Assistant sends and when. The unit tests pin the decisions; they cannot pin that
`announcement_in_progress` is on the wire, or that a real announcement now plays to
its last word. Worth watching for on a device: an announcement over a video (the
ducking path can only ever have run since the classifier started working), and a
pause still falling silent at once.
