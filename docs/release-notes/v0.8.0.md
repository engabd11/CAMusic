v0.8.0 - the Navidrome half stops being the second-class half
Stable build of CAMusic for Android.

## Install
1. Download **camusic-0.8.0.apk** below (Android 12 / API 31+).
2. Allow **install from unknown sources**, then open it. Installs over 0.7.x (versionCode 32) — same signing key, so your servers, credentials and downloads survive.

Nine items were planned for this release and seven landed whole; see
[docs/v0.8-plan.md](../v0.8-plan.md), which carries the plan and a table of exactly what
shipped against it. The theme underneath most of it: a surprising amount was already
written and simply never called.

## The Navidrome player keeps going

Radio mode and "don't stop the music" are Music Assistant *server* features, so the
standalone backend has always just stopped at the end of the album — the backend you
reach for when MA is down, which is exactly when you least want the room to go quiet. It
builds the next batch itself now.

The ladder runs strongest claim first and stops at the first rung that answers: tracks
like this one, then tracks from around this record, then the artist's best-known songs,
then the genre, then anything at all. A plain Subsonic server with no last.fm metadata
falls through the first four every time, which is why the floor exists — a radio that
plays nothing is not a radio. A rolling history stops it circling back within a session,
and what is already queued is filtered out.

The top-up happens two tracks before the end rather than at it. ExoPlayer is handed the
whole queue at once and that is what makes its transitions gapless; waiting for the queue
to empty would mean a silence, a fetch and a fresh prepare.

Offline is its own path: with no server there is no similarity to ask about, so it ranks
your downloads by genre and artist and shuffles, which is the most that can honestly be
done.

**Smooth transitions** come with it, and are deliberately not called crossfade — one
ExoPlayer has one output, so two tracks cannot overlap through it. This fades one out and
the next in, 1–12 seconds, off by default. It suppresses itself while the queue is a
single album, because a record is sequenced and fading between its tracks damages it.
Queue something from off the record and the fade comes back.

## The quality badge says what you are actually hearing

The badge reads `FLAC • 96/24 • 3 Mb/s` now, with channel count and file size in the
card. The bitrate and channels were already being parsed off OpenSubsonic and thrown
away.

The card also stops shoving the album art around when it opens — it was a `Popup` inside
the layout, which is the structural fault behind two separate layout bugs, and it is an
overlay sibling now.

And it says what Music Assistant did to the level. MA reports its loudness handling per
track in a field the app was parsing and discarding, so a track that had been normalised
looked identical to one that had not.

## The MA DSP stops failing silently

Saving on the DSP screen reported success and changed nothing you could hear. Both calls
were swallowing the server's error. The screen surfaces what the server actually said
now, and `DSPDetails.state` — which MA reports per track, and which the app was already
parsing — is shown in the panel and on the quality card.

That is the diagnosis half of the problem. Whether the DSP then *applies* depends on
answers only a device against a real MA can give, and those are still open.

## Album and artist screens say more than a track list

`music/artists/top_tracks` and `music/artists/similar_artists` had been sitting in the
repository with zero callers. On the Navidrome side `getArtistInfo2` was being asked for
`count=0` similar artists — the app explicitly telling the server not to send the thing
it wanted — and only the biography was read out of a response that also carries
MusicBrainz and last.fm links.

Albums get an About section and related albums; artists get top tracks and similar
artists. Three parsing gaps were fixed on the way through.

## The library searches as you type

Search fired only on the IME Search key, with no debounce anywhere in the tree. It is
debounced and `flatMapLatest`ed now, results are held across keystrokes rather than
blanking, and an LRU cache means backspacing is instant instead of a fresh round trip.

## Lyrics that keep up

The sung line sits in the middle of the pane instead of two rows from the top, scrolls
there smoothly, and scales into place rather than jumping 16→20sp. There is a 200 ms lead
so the line arrives with the voice, a trim in Settings for a server whose timings are
off, and the remote position tick is up to 250 ms so a remote player's lyrics move as
evenly as a local one's.

## Light Sync gets its own settings tab

Transport and pairing were buried inside Servers. They have a section of their own now,
and the Home Assistant credentials moved with them — the card is the transport picker's
`else` branch, so splitting them would have meant duplicating it. The transport also
follows the library backend immediately rather than after a restart.

And the engine reads the track before it starts: the show knows the song's shape from the
first bar instead of learning it as it goes.

## Also

- **24-bit reaches the Navidrome player**, behind the bit-perfect setting.
- **Pick up where another device left off.** `getPlayQueue` and `savePlayQueue` had no
  callers; cross-device resume was UI, not protocol.
- **Choose which copy of a track to play.** Same story — `music/tracks/track_versions`
  already knew about the 16/44 stream, the 24/96 purchase and the CD rip.
- State collection across the app follows the lifecycle now, so screens off the top of
  the stack stop doing work.

## Not in this one

Android Auto, the widget and the QS tile, a real release keystore, Room, instrumented
tests and crash reporting are untouched — as are native AAudio, MA-path gapless and the
USB DAC readouts. They are sequenced in the plan document rather than dropped.
