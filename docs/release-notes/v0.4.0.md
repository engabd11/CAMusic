## What's new since v0.3.7

This release came out of an audit rather than a bug report: every command, message and
endpoint the app sends, checked against the Music Assistant 2.9.9 commands reference,
the published Sendspin specification, and the Subsonic/Navidrome documentation. Where
the code and the specification disagreed, the specification won — and they disagreed
more often than expected.

### The last second of every track was being thrown away

Music Assistant sends `stream/end` and then `stream/start` between **every** track, not
just when playback stops. `stream/end` released the AudioTrack, and it did so with
`flush()` — which **discards** whatever PCM has not been played yet, out of a buffer
deliberately sized to about one second. Then MediaCodec and the AudioTrack were rebuilt
from scratch for the next track.

So the end of every track was cut off, and there was a rebuild gap after it. On the app's
primary playback path. Every continuous album — a live recording, a DJ mix, anything
crossfaded, most classical — was audibly wrong.

`stream/end` now only *marks* the stream finished. The tail keeps playing, and if the
next `stream/start` describes the same format the whole thing carries straight on
through it. Teardown drains rather than discards: the decoder is given an explicit
end-of-stream, then `AudioTrack.stop()` (which plays out, unlike `flush()`), then a
bounded wait on the playback head — because `stop()` drains asynchronously and releasing
underneath it would truncate anyway.

The reuse decision deliberately answers two questions, not one. Music Assistant's
per-track FLAC `codec_header` is a STREAMINFO block carrying that track's own sample
count and MD5, so it differs at every boundary even when the *format* has not changed.
The decoder gets rebuilt; the AudioTrack — which is holding the tail — does not.

Keeping a track alive broke an assumption the scheduler had been getting for free: with
a fresh track per stream it was always empty when the first frame was written. Now a
sample handed over is not heard until what is queued ahead of it has played, so the wait
subtracts what is already buffered. It reads zero on a fresh track, so a cold start
behaves exactly as it did.

### Creating and deleting playlists never worked

`music/playlists/create`, `music/playlists/delete` and `music/playlists/edit` are not
Music Assistant commands. They do not appear anywhere in the 2.9.9 reference. The real
names are `create_playlist`, `remove` and `update` — and `remove` takes a bare
`item_id`, not the `item_id` + provider pair every other command takes, so the argument
shape was wrong as well as the name.

Both shipped with new UI in v0.3.7, which means the feature has never worked against any
server since the day it was added.

The same audit found two more on the Navidrome side. `updatePlaylist` was being sent
`songId`, a parameter it does not have — it takes `songIdToAdd` and `songIndexToRemove`,
a delta, where the removal is an *index into the playlist* rather than a song id. And
repeated parameters were being comma-joined, so `songId=a,b,c` reached the server as one
id with commas in it.

### Adding a track to a playlist

Neither backend could do it. A playlist could be created and deleted, and then filled
only by playing into it and saving the queue. Long-press a track or an album →
**Add to playlist** → pick one.

### The Navidrome and offline player is now ExoPlayer

`android.media.MediaPlayer` could not do the things this audience notices.

- **Gapless.** `setNextMediaPlayer` was the framework's only hook and its behaviour is
  OEM-dependent, so an album could gap on one phone and not another. The whole queue
  goes to ExoPlayer at once now, which is what lets it buffer across a boundary.
- **ReplayGain is applied.** The tags were being read off OpenSubsonic and printed on
  the quality card while nothing acted on them — the number on screen described
  something that was not happening, and a 1985 master and a 2015 one still landed 10 dB
  apart in one playlist. Off / track / album in Settings, defaulting to album, which is
  the right answer for anyone who listens to records: it levels albums against each
  other without flattening the quiet passages inside one. Boosts are capped at +3 dB,
  because a boost multiplies samples already mastered against full scale and has
  nowhere to go but the ceiling.
- **Exact seek.** MediaPlayer seeks to the nearest sync sample, which in a
  twenty-minute movement can be seconds from where your finger went.
- **Float output**, so 24-bit sources are not quietly requantised on the way to the mixer.
- A buffer sized for lossless-over-LAN instead of the video-on-mobile-data default.
- A failed track no longer ends the album.

### Headphones out no longer plays the room

`ACTION_AUDIO_BECOMING_NOISY` was handled on neither path, so unplugging headphones or
dropping a Bluetooth link moved the music to the phone's own speaker at whatever volume
had just been in your ears. The local player gets this from ExoPlayer; the Sendspin path
asks the *server* to pause, because the queue belongs to Music Assistant and going quiet
locally would leave the rest of a group playing on.

### Three more Sendspin specification violations

- **Wire ordering.** Control JSON and binary audio share one socket, and `stream/clear`
  only means anything if it is still ordered against the audio around it. They were
  being handled through separate flows in separate coroutines, so a seek could be
  applied *after* the frames it was meant to discard — a burst of pre-seek audio.
  Both now feed one queue drained by one consumer.
- **The sync trim did nothing.** `static_delay_ms` was read by the audio scheduler and
  written by nothing at all: the per-speaker delay set on the Speakers screen went only
  to Music Assistant's config and never reached the thing that schedules frames. Wired
  end to end, including the `set_static_delay` command the server can push.
- **The player claimed to be synchronised before it was.** `client/state` reported
  `"synchronized"` from the moment the socket opened. The specification says an
  unsynchronised client reports `"error"` and mutes while it keeps buffering — and the
  clock filter already knew when it had converged. In a group, this phone was joining
  before its offset had settled.

### Smaller

- A dropped Music Assistant socket resolved pending requests to `null`, so a library
  that *failed* to load looked exactly like a library that is *empty*. It fails now, and
  only genuine transport failures are retried — a server rejection was being re-sent
  repeatedly to get the same answer.
- Subsonic error codes were interpolated into a message and never read, so a wrong
  password and an unreachable host reported identically. Rejected credentials now keep
  the connect form up instead of dropping to the offline library.
- "Play at original quality" never scrobbled — the one path this audience uses most,
  untouched and straight from the source, was the one that never reached play counts.
- An OpenSubsonic lyric starting at exactly 0 ms lost its timestamp and was then
  dropped; a plain lyric set listed before a synced one won.
- Six view models each built their own HTTP client against one server. Now one.
- 176.4 and 192 kHz are advertised — but only behind a device probe, since Music
  Assistant may only send a format the client listed.
- New: `music/tracks/track_versions` and `music/albums/album_versions` (every copy of a
  track across every provider), `players/cmd/volume_mute` (real mute, which restores the
  previous level), `setRating`, `getPlayQueue`/`savePlayQueue`, `getOpenSubsonicExtensions`.

## Tests

167 unit tests, all passing — 36 new. `StreamContinuityTest` covers the track-reuse
decision, `SchedulingTest` the buffered-audio arithmetic that makes it correct,
`SyncGateTest` the convergence reporting, `ReplayGainTest` the gain and its clipping
cap, plus the ordered inbox, the Subsonic error codes and the lyrics parser.

## Not verified on device

**None of the audio work in this release has been run against real hardware.** The
diagnoses are grounded in the specifications and the arithmetic is unit-tested, but
gapless is a thing you confirm with ears. Worth checking:

1. A continuous album — a live record, a DJ mix — has **no gap and no clipped tail**
   between tracks over Music Assistant.
2. Seeking repeatedly mid-track never plays a burst of the old position.
3. In a group, the phone stays silent until its clock converges rather than joining
   early and out of step; and a sync delay audibly shifts it. **The sign of that
   control is worth checking against a second speaker** — the specification's sense
   ("this output adds latency, so play earlier") is the opposite of how Music
   Assistant's own signed control reads, and the two could not be reconciled from the
   code alone.
4. Unplugging headphones pauses on both backends.
5. On Navidrome: a gapless album, ReplayGain audibly different between off and album,
   and a USB DAC still routed.

One known risk: what Music Assistant does with a player reporting `state: "error"` is
not knowable from this repository. It may drop the player from the speaker list for the
couple of seconds a cold connection takes to converge. If that happens, say so — the
fallback is to report it only when actually grouped.
